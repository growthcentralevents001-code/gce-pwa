# FD-031 — GCE Connect AI Lead Assist Architecture

**Decision ID:** FD-031
**Title:** GCE Connect AI Lead Assist Architecture
**Status:** Founder Approved
**Decision Type:** AI-Assisted Lead Intelligence, Routing, Expert Selection, Opportunity Management, Rights Protection, and Monetisation Constitution
**Authority Level:** Founder Decision
**Platform:** Growth Central Events (GCE)
**Applies To:** GCE Connect, GCE Marketplace, GCE Enterprise, GCE Connect Networking, GCE Connect Offer Events, future GCE Connect Marketing, future GCE Connect Travel, Lead Givers, Lead Receivers, customers, Circle Members, Connect BDPs, Marketplace BDPs, Venue Partners, Enterprise BDPs, Enterprise Platform Experts, GCE Lead Intelligence and Opportunity Desk, Governing Bodies, vendors, collaborators, and platform administrators

---

## 1. Purpose

This Founder Decision defines the architecture, rights, responsibilities, workflows, safeguards, monetisation principles, and cross-vertical operating model of GCE Connect AI Lead Assist.

It establishes:

- A central platform-driven Lead Intelligence Engine
- A platform-retained expert team
- Basic protected lead rights
- AI-assisted lead verification, classification, matching, routing, reassignment, and tracking
- Human oversight and explainability
- Cross-vertical routing into Connect, Marketplace, Enterprise, and future sub-verticals
- Lead Giver and Lead Receiver protections
- Customer consent and privacy controls
- Expert-assisted selection and opportunity-management services
- High-value monetisation without pay-to-win routing
- Dashboard, notification, audit, and reporting requirements
- Launch-stage product levels
- Matters that remain unresolved and must not be invented

This decision must be read together with FD-001, FD-020, FD-021, FD-023, FD-024, FD-025, FD-026, FD-027, FD-028, FD-029, and FD-030.

# PART A — CORE DEFINITION AND PRINCIPLES

## 2. Definition

GCE Connect AI Lead Assist is:

> A platform-controlled opportunity-assistance system that helps classify, verify, match, route, track, coordinate, and improve business opportunities among eligible GCE stakeholders.

It is not:

- A guaranteed lead-selling service
- A guaranteed revenue service
- An autonomous contract-awarding system
- A substitute for human commercial judgment
- A hidden pay-to-win routing engine
- A credit-rating system
- A replacement for Circle relationship-building
- A right to buy another member’s protected opportunity

## 3. Central Architecture

GCE shall operate:

> **One central GCE Lead Intelligence Engine with vertical-specific routing, verification, commercial, fulfilment, commission, and settlement rules.**

The shared intelligence layer may support all GCE verticals and sub-verticals, but each vertical retains its own approved commercial and operational rules.

## 4. Core Human-Control Principle

AI may recommend, classify, rank, route, flag, and assist.

AI may not independently:

- Accept a contract
- Set a member’s price
- Award a project
- Transfer money
- Approve refund or settlement
- Suspend or terminate membership
- Permanently reduce Trust Rank
- Grant or deny Core Tier
- Decide serious disputes
- Determine legal liability
- Override platform taxonomy without authority

Final business acceptance, rejection, negotiation, pricing, contracting, fulfilment, dispute resolution, and commercial responsibility remain human-controlled.

# PART B — PLATFORM EXPERT TEAM

## 5. GCE Lead Intelligence and Opportunity Desk

GCE shall retain a dedicated internal or directly controlled team known as:

> **GCE Lead Intelligence and Opportunity Desk**

The team may include:

- Lead Verification Experts
- Business Matching Experts
- Sector Specialists
- Opportunity Coordinators
- Data and AI Analysts
- Compliance and Privacy Reviewers
- Customer Requirement Specialists
- Lead Dispute and Attribution Reviewers

## 6. Expert Team Status

The expert team:

- Is retained, employed, or directly controlled by GCE
- Is not an independent franchise layer
- Does not own leads
- Does not receive hidden personal commission from selected members
- Operates under role-based access
- Must follow confidentiality, conflict-of-interest, privacy, audit, and separation-of-duties controls

## 7. Expert Team Authority

The team may:

- Verify requirements
- Clarify lead information
- Prepare requirement briefs
- Recommend classifications
- Prepare shortlists
- Review low-confidence AI outputs
- Coordinate customer discussions
- Recommend collaboration
- Escalate suitable opportunities to Marketplace or Enterprise
- Review fraud, privacy, duplicate, attribution, and routing disputes
- Support managed opportunities

The team may not:

- Secretly favour a member
- Sell customer data
- Accept a contract for a member
- Set a member’s pricing
- Guarantee provider performance
- Override taxonomy without authority
- Approve its own commercial exception
- Receive undisclosed personal benefit from selection

## 8. Expert Team Performance

Performance should be measured using:

- Verification accuracy
- Routing accuracy
- Response time
- Customer satisfaction
- Dispute rate
- Privacy compliance
- Conversion quality
- Cross-vertical routing quality
- Collaboration success
- Audit completeness

Performance should not be measured only by transaction value.

# PART C — PROTECTED CORE LEAD RIGHTS

## 9. Basic Protected Member Functionality

The following remain protected core functionality for active eligible members:

- Giving a genuine referral
- Receiving an ordinary relevant referral
- Lead-source attribution
- Accepting or declining a lead
- Requesting clarification
- Reporting duplicate or invalid leads
- Recording follow-up
- Participating in Dual-Confirmed Closed Business
- Viewing basic status
- Raising a dispute
- Receiving fair routing based on approved eligibility rules

These rights must not require purchase of a premium package.

## 10. Lead Giver Rights

The Lead Giver is protected through:

- Permanent preservation of original source
- Submission timestamp
- Attribution history
- No silent replacement
- No deletion of original assignment history
- Free dispute access
- Protection from paid services transferring their credit
- Dual-confirmed closed-business recognition
- Privacy controls over shared customer data

Lead Giver, verifier, Lead Receiver, selected provider, closer, and commercial beneficiary must remain separately identifiable.

## 11. Lead Giver Benefits

The Lead Giver benefits through:

- Formal recognition
- Transparent tracking
- Reduced risk of lead misuse
- Better follow-up
- Expert clarification
- Correct Specialization routing
- Collaboration support
- Official closed-business attribution

Lead submission does not automatically create commission unless separately approved.

## 12. Lead Receiver Rights

The Lead Receiver is protected through:

- Fair and explainable routing
- No hidden paid priority
- Clear lead-quality status
- Clear response deadline
- Ability to accept, decline, clarify, report duplicate, report invalid, or request collaboration
- No charge merely to receive an ordinary valid Circle referral
- No retroactive fee
- No undisclosed success fee
- Human review of incorrect AI routing
- Customer-consent checks
- Reassignment history
- Valid decline reasons without automatic penalty

## 13. Lead Receiver Benefits

The Lead Receiver benefits through:

- Better-quality and more relevant leads
- Fewer fake or duplicate enquiries
- Improved requirement clarity
- Follow-up tools
- Pipeline tracking
- Expert coordination
- Collaboration opportunities
- Cross-Circle and cross-city opportunities
- Marketplace or Enterprise escalation where appropriate
- Optional advanced tools and analytics

## 14. Customer Rights

The customer is protected through:

- Consent or lawful basis before contact sharing
- Purpose limitation
- Limited recipient access
- Withdrawal rights
- No bulk marketing without consent
- No sale of customer data
- Provider choice
- Human review for sensitive or high-value cases
- Transparent paid-service disclosure
- Complaint access
- Data-retention controls

## 15. Customer Benefits

The customer benefits through:

- Faster access to verified providers
- Requirement clarification
- Capability and licence checks
- Expert shortlisting
- Proposal coordination
- Multi-member solutions
- Marketplace or Enterprise escalation

# PART F — LEAD QUALITY AND VERIFICATION

## 24. Lead Quality States

Approved states:

1. Unverified Lead
2. Preliminarily Verified Lead
3. Qualified Lead
4. Rejected / Invalid Lead

## 25. Minimum Qualification Conditions

A lead should not become Qualified unless:

- A genuine requirement exists
- Contact permission is valid
- Information is sufficiently complete
- The category is permitted
- Location is identifiable where relevant
- The lead is not a known duplicate
- The lead is not closed, expired, or withdrawn
- The submitter has authority or valid basis to share it
- No obvious privacy or platform-policy conflict exists

High-value, regulated, sensitive, disputed, or low-confidence leads require human review.

## 26. Verification Methods

Verification may use:

- OTP
- Phone or email confirmation
- Platform identity
- Member confirmation
- Customer confirmation
- Requirement document
- Appointment confirmation
- Human verification call
- Duplicate screening
- Fraud screening
- Historical behaviour signals
- Enterprise project verification

No single automated signal is absolute proof.

# PART G — AI CLASSIFICATION AND ROUTING

## 27. AI Classification

AI may identify:

- Correct GCE vertical
- Business Specialization
- Relevant Tags
- GC Power Sector
- Geography
- Budget category
- Urgency
- Complexity
- Required licences
- Single-provider or multi-provider need
- Connect, Marketplace, Enterprise, or future sub-vertical destination

Low-confidence outputs require human review.

## 28. Routing Hierarchy

Routing should consider:

1. Regulatory and eligibility checks
2. Correct vertical
3. Business Specialization
4. Protected Tag Scope
5. Relevant approved Tags
6. Geography
7. Availability
8. Capacity
9. Response performance
10. Referral relevance
11. Duplicate and conflict checks
12. Customer preference
13. Human review where required

Advertising, sponsorship, premium visibility, and workshop purchases must not override core eligibility.

## 29. Circle-First Routing

For ordinary local Connect opportunities:

1. Eligible member in source Circle
2. Eligible member in nearby active Circle
3. Eligible member in same city
4. Eligible member in another city where appropriate
5. Marketplace or Enterprise where the opportunity belongs elsewhere

## 30. Assignment Types

A lead may be:

- Exclusive
- Limited distribution
- Open distribution
- Multi-member collaboration
- Enterprise-managed

No automatic exclusivity applies unless approved conditions are met.

## 31. Member Eligibility

A member should normally be eligible only if:

- Membership is active
- Verification is current
- Relevant Specialization or Tag is active
- Required licences are valid
- No serious compliance hold exists
- Member is not suspended
- Geography is serviceable
- Capacity is available
- Lead rules are accepted
- No serious misuse pattern exists

# PART L — FRAUD, DISPUTES, AND HUMAN OVERSIGHT

## 45. Fraud Indicators

Possible indicators include:

- Fake contact details
- Repeated duplicate submissions
- Self-created leads
- Circular referrals
- Artificial conversion reporting
- Customer impersonation
- Paid lead reselling
- Manipulated values
- Unauthorised harvesting
- False rejection claims
- Coordinated routing abuse

AI may flag risk, but serious action requires human review.

## 46. Dispute Categories

Disputes may concern:

- Wrong routing
- Duplicate lead
- Lead already known
- Fake lead
- Missing consent
- Customer did not request service
- Wrong Specialization
- Unfair reassignment
- Multiple claimant dispute
- Incorrect closure attribution
- Data misuse
- False conversion report

## 47. Dispute Workflow

1. Dispute submitted
2. Appropriate hold applied
3. Evidence collected
4. Parties notified
5. Response opportunity
6. Human review
7. Decision and reason
8. Routing or attribution corrected
9. Appeal where permitted
10. Audit preserved

AI does not issue final decisions in serious disputes.

## 48. Mandatory Human Review

Human review is required where:

- AI confidence is low
- Lead value is high
- Opportunity is regulated
- Privacy concern exists
- Fraud signal exists
- Multiple parties claim priority
- Customer disputes routing
- Classification is challenged
- Enterprise escalation is possible
- Restriction or suspension is proposed

# PART R — USE CASES

## 77. Ordinary Circle Referral

A Circle member submits a customer requirement for a chartered accountant.

Flow:

1. Consent recorded
2. AI classifies the requirement
3. Eligible Circle member identified
4. Lead offered
5. Member accepts
6. Business completed
7. Lead Giver enters closed-business value
8. Lead Receiver confirms
9. Official ledger updated

No fee applies merely for the ordinary referral.

## 78. Specialization Overlap

A solar-installation requirement is incorrectly submitted under Electrical Contractor.

Flow:

1. AI detects overlap
2. Protected Tag Scope checked
3. Human expert reviews
4. Lead routed to the member with correct verified capability

## 79. Marketplace Venue Booking

A customer needs a banquet venue.

Flow:

1. Date, city, capacity, and budget collected
2. Marketplace classification
3. Verified venues matched
4. Customer selects venue
5. Booking fulfilled
6. Marketplace settlement follows FD-028 and FD-029

## 80. Enterprise Escalation

A company needs a multi-city office setup.

Flow:

1. Original Lead Giver attribution preserved
2. AI identifies Enterprise characteristics
3. Enterprise Platform Expert prepares brief
4. Vendors and members shortlisted
5. Milestones tracked
6. Enterprise commercial rules apply

## 81. Multi-Member Collaboration

A restaurant launch requires multiple Specializations.

Flow:

1. AI identifies required capabilities
2. Expert team verifies scope
3. Collaboration team formed
4. Customer consent recorded
5. Scope-specific access assigned
6. Outcomes attributed separately

## 82. Duplicate Lead

Two members submit the same customer requirement.

Flow:

1. Possible duplicate flagged
2. Original records preserved
3. Timestamps and consent checked
4. Customer confirms
5. Decision recorded
6. Attribution corrected if required

## 83. Valid Decline

A member has no capacity.

Flow:

1. Member selects No Capacity
2. No automatic Trust Rank penalty
3. Lead reassigned
4. Availability improves future routing

## 84. Paid Expert Selection

A customer requests GCE to shortlist commercial architects.

Flow:

1. Basic lead remains free
2. Customer purchases Expert Selection
3. Requirement and credentials verified
4. Eligible members shortlisted
5. Customer selects provider
6. GCE earns disclosed service fee

## 85. Lead Assist Pro

A member purchases advanced tools such as pipeline, reminders, analytics, and team access.

The purchase improves productivity but does not buy routing priority.

## 94. Decision Statement

GCE shall operate one central GCE Lead Intelligence Engine supported by the GCE Lead Intelligence and Opportunity Desk.

Basic lead giving, ordinary eligible lead receipt, attribution protection, acceptance, decline, clarification, follow-up, and dispute access remain protected core functionality.

GCE may monetise enhanced verification, expert selection, managed opportunity services, collaboration coordination, advanced tools, analytics, institutional processing, and cross-vertical execution support.

Paid services must not override Business Specialization, Protected Tag Scope, customer preference, geography, compliance, fair routing, privacy, or legitimate Lead Giver and Lead Receiver rights.

AI shall remain assistive. Human oversight shall remain mandatory for low-confidence, high-value, regulated, disputed, privacy-sensitive, fraud-flagged, or commercially complex opportunities.

The feature may support all GCE verticals and sub-verticals through one shared intelligence layer while each vertical retains its own approved commercial, fulfilment, commission, and settlement rules.

This decision remains active until explicitly amended or superseded by a later Founder Decision.
